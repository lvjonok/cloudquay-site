Privacy

Your connections stay yours.

Effective October 26, 2026. This policy describes the personal-use CloudQuay macOS app.

Information the app accesses

With your authorization, CloudQuay accesses Google Drive folder and file metadata and the content of files you open or download. Read-only authorization allows listing and downloading. Read-and-write authorization also allows the file changes you perform through the mounted drive, including uploading, renaming, and deleting files.

The app accesses NAS files through connections you configure. On initial setup, it can import existing connection labels, addresses, and usernames from local connection preferences. It does not import another application's passwords or Google authorization.

Use and sharing

CloudQuay uses this information to show your storage in Finder and perform file operations you request. It does not sell your Google data, use it for advertising, or use it to train machine-learning models. It has no application analytics or developer-operated file-processing server. Its use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

The app communicates with Google for sign-in and Drive requests, and with the NAS servers you configure. The rclone helper and macOS filesystem components run locally. Google and your storage provider process their own service requests under their respective policies.

Local storage and security

Connection settings, Google authorization tokens, diagnostic logs, and cached file data are stored on your Mac. NAS passwords are stored in the macOS Keychain. Google tokens are currently stored in a private rclone configuration file. Accessed file content and pending writes use a private persistent cache. These files rely on your Mac's account permissions and disk protection; CloudQuay does not add a separate encryption layer.

Retention and removal

Unused cached content has a 24-hour age target and a configurable size target. Open files and changes awaiting upload can remain longer. Eject connections and confirm uploads have finished before deleting cached data.

Disconnecting or removing a connection does not automatically revoke Google's authorization or erase its saved account configuration. Revoke CloudQuay access in your Google Account connections to stop future access. After safely disconnecting, you can remove local CloudQuay configuration and cache files from ~/Library/Application Support/CloudQuay/ and CloudQuay password entries from Keychain Access. Files in your Google Drive or NAS remain with that provider.

Your control

Authorization is optional. You can choose read-only access, disconnect drives, revoke Google access, and remove local app data. Each person who uses the app controls their own storage authorization.
